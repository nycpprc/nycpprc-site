## 3. Legal risks flagged
1. Sole-member control. Lawful under N-PCL (one class with one member is permitted) and not a bar to 501(c)(3) or public-charity status (public support tests look at funding sources, not governance). But Ryan is simultaneously sole member, director, chair and likely president, holds the Meetup group personally, and may be paid as a host — the classic profile the IRS and NY AG examine for inurement and private benefit. Mitigations drafted: recusal everywhere he is interested, disinterested-director approvals with §715(b) documentation, two-signature payments, successor-member clause. Strongly consider adding independent directors to make a majority independent.
2. Reserved powers. Amendments, merger, dissolution and asset sales already need member approval by statute; budget approval is a contractual limit on board management (N-PCL §701) — flagged for counsel.
3. Chair tie-break. N-PCL §708(d) majority-of-present rule; a casting vote may not be valid — two alternatives drafted.
4. Interim executive action. Executive committee needs 3+ directors (§712); drafted as capped officer authority with ratification; related party matters excluded because ratification is only a limited defense (§715(j)).
5. Donation-linked host pay. Revenue-based compensation to insiders is a recognized inurement/excess-benefit risk (People of God; Treas. Reg. §53.4958-4(a)(4)); the cap, lesser-of formula, corporate control of donations and comparability data mitigate it; a flat stipend from general funds is lower risk. Donor-facing: donations must be to the corporation, not earmarked for hosts (Rev. Rul. 62-113).
6. 1023-EZ eligibility. Gross receipts include every donation; five events a week could approach the $50,000 ceiling. Worksheet Q9 (successor to a for-profit) should be confirmed given the group's history under Ryan personally. False attestation risks later revocation.
7. Meetup license. Revocable license of a personally owned asset means the charity builds value it doesn't own (private benefit to Ryan; program-continuity risk). Drafted: right of first offer, succession instruction, corporate ownership of its own data/accounts, transition notice, non-compete/good-faith covenant (enforceability flagged). Check Meetup Terms on organizations and data export.
8. Therapy practice. Private benefit if events or lists funnel clients to the practice; drafted prohibitions plus a no-clinical-services clause in the certificate.
9. Worker classification. 1099 treatment is fact-specific; NY tests can be stricter.
10. Educational purposes. No Education Department consent is needed because the corporation won't run a school/library/museum, but the §404(d) certified-copy notice to NYSED within 30 business days IS required.
11. Pre-incorporation funds/assets held personally should be transferred and documented; stop collecting donations to personal accounts before CHAR410 registration and solicitation.
12. Type A/B/C/D classification: abolished by the Nonprofit Revitalization Act of 2013 (effective July 1, 2014); the current N-PCL requires the certificate to say 'charitable' or 'non-charitable' under §201. The draft uses 'charitable corporation'.
